# My Story: From Nepal to New York City

*A reflection on my journey in Data Science at Columbia.*

If someone had asked me five years ago, "Where do you see yourself five years from now?", my answer would have been nowhere close to “a Data Science graduate from an Ivy League Institution living in New York City” or “having developed an obsession over matcha latte” or “learning and implementing agentic AI solutions”. Five years back, I never envisioned myself becoming an Ivy League graduate, let alone moving to New York City and starting a new chapter all by myself. 

After receiving an admit from the University of Washington for graduate studies, I decided to pack all my things the following week and move to Seattle. But, the universe had different plans for me. One late afternoon around 5 pm, I got an email with an acceptance letter from Columbia! I called my dad right away at four in the morning (Nepal time), and he was thrilled that I had made it. My life took a pivot, and I decided to risk it all: my time, energy, and money to invest in my education. I decided to bet on my capabilities. 

In my first semester, I had a lot of adjusting to do. The core classes were rigorous and honestly, truly overwhelming. But I knew this is what I had signed up for. One of the hardest classes was Algorithms, but somewhere along the way I realized that not all theories and problems require cracking codes. For example, NP-complete problems can also be seen through philosophical lenses. Let’s think about it this way- The NP complete problem teaches us that some problems may be fundamentally intractable, no matter how hard we try. Unless P = NP, there may never exist efficient algorithms for a given problem. So, my first semester was mostly observing and trying to make it all sense to the best of my abilities. 

My second semester at Columbia was when I really explored and immersed myself into learning deep statistics and machine learning. Instead of looking at assignments as a burden or a checkbox in my to-do list, I found it as a channel to explore the topics I liked, found intriguing, and to keep learning. I started noticing how topics like probability distribution functions, which I learned in my statistics course, came to real life applications in my finance elective. I also found joy in attending Machine Learning class lectures. People sitting left and right of me were posing questions that would have never run through my mind. So, I felt a deep sense of gratitude to be in that lecture room.

During summer, I interned at Diligent, where I got to implement a lot of things I learned from my classes at Columbia. I learned two most important things: approximately 75~80% of data science involves data cleaning, preprocessing, and feature engineering, and another, having a great snack station in the office really keeps people coming in person despite a hybrid work setting. Jokes apart, I learned that while class assignments follow a specific structure, the real world does not. For example, in an assignment, we are instructed to solve an optimization problem in a particular way (using a formula, process, or rule), but in a professional setting, we need to determine the concrete steps using our own judgment. I also realized that as an engineer, building for business is more important than building “big”. 

In my third semester, I found myself in a unique position where I had a lot of opportunities to give back what I had learned. I was invited to several panel discussions to mentor my juniors who wanted to know about my internship and academic experience. I also got a Teaching Assistant role for Calculus class, which I had taken during my undergraduate. This position prompted me to go back to my academic timeline, revise Calculus concepts, and teach undergraduate students from scratch. 

As I have already graduated and am officially an alum, upon looking back, the past two years at Columbia have been deeply inspirational to me. Working and learning alongside talented peers and professors has continued to inspire me to learn, grow, and evolve in Data Science. Today, one of my favorite things to do is to grab a matcha latte, head to the Butler library, work on personal projects, and just simply learn. Columbia brought out a version of me that I never knew existed. This version of me loves building Data Science and AI systems. Here, I learned to juggle, hustle, and innovate. I could not be more proud and happy to call this place - the heart of innovation in New York City - my alma mater.
